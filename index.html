📖 Theoretical Concepts

Fundamental Principles of Cheminformatics & Computational Drug Design

1. Name-to-SMILES Conversion

SMILES (Simplified Molecular-Input Line-Entry System) represents chemical structures using short ASCII strings. When you enter a common drug name (such as Paracetamol or Ibuprofen), this virtual lab queries chemical databases to resolve its molecular graph and output its unique Canonical SMILES code.

  • Atoms: Represented by chemical symbols (C, N, O, Cl).
  • Bonds: Single bonds are implied or -, double bonds are =, triple bonds are #.
  • Branches: Enclosed inside parentheses, e.g., CC(=O)O for Acetic Acid.
  • Ring Structures: Numbers indicate ring closures, e.g., C1=CC=CC=C1 for Benzene.

2. Lipinski's Rule of Five (Drug-Likeness)

Lipinski's Rule of Five evaluates whether a chemical compound has properties that make it likely to be an orally active drug in humans. A compound passes if it has no more than 1 violation of these rules:

  • Molecular Weight (MW): ≤ 500 g/mol
  • Lipophilicity (XLogP): ≤ 5
  • Hydrogen Bond Donors (HBD): ≤ 5
  • Hydrogen Bond Acceptors (HBA): ≤ 10
